Where the files are

Two routes

  1. The receipt email

    Payhip sends it within a minute or two of payment, from a payhip.com address, carrying a download link for every item in your order. Keep the email, and read the note below about how many times that link will work.

  2. The confirmation screen you just came from

    It offered the same download before it sent you here. If your browser still has it in history, that link works too.

The link works five times

Your download link is good for five downloads, which is enough to get the packet onto a school machine, a home machine and a backup drive with room to spare. It is not enough to serve as your permanent copy, so treat the fifth one as the last.

So do this now, while you are thinking about it: download the files and put them in a folder you control, on your own drive, rather than leaving them as a link in your inbox. There is no account here and nothing to log back into. That is deliberate. Once the files are on your drive they are yours outright, nobody can take them away from you, and nobody is holding a spare copy for you either.

Run out anyway? A machine died, a folder got cleaned out, the download failed halfway three times running. Write to Inquiries@fictionlaboratory.com and the count gets reset or the files get sent to you directly. You bought the packet. The limit is there to stop the link being passed around, not to stop you having what you paid for, and nobody is going to make you argue for it.

If the email has not arrived

Four things to try

  1. Give it five minutes

    Payment processors and mail servers do not always agree about what counts as fast.

  2. Look in spam or junk

    A first message from a sender you have never received mail from lands there routinely.

  3. If you bought this on a school address, suspect the district filter

    Many school systems quarantine mail from unfamiliar senders before it reaches a teacher's inbox, and a first message from Payhip is exactly that. Check the quarantine digest your district sends, or ask whoever runs your mail to release it. Buying on a personal address is the reliable way around the problem.

  4. Check the address you typed at checkout

    A transposed letter sends the packet somewhere real and silent.

Still nothing? Write to Inquiries@fictionlaboratory.com with the name and email on the order and the files will be sent to you directly. That address is also the one to use if you have used up your five downloads and need the packet again. A person reads it, and no email you send there costs you a download.

What is in the folder

Eight documents

01 · Specimen record

The catalog entry: title, author, year, discipline codes and a plot summary, on one page.

02 · Teacher introduction

Read this one first. What the packet holds, how it is meant to run, and anything to know before the class meets the book.

03 · Reader introduction

The page a student reads first, explaining what they are being asked to do and why.

04 · Sources

The research behind the packet, in MLA style, most of it free to read.

05 · Reading companion

Four sections: the author, the moment the book was written into, what the story is really about, and the science, with its two audits.

06 · Quiz, three variants

The objective questions differ across A, B and C so answers cannot be traded. The short answers stay the same, so one rubric covers the class.

07 · Answer guide

Keys for all three variants, plus model responses and grading notes for the short answers.

08 · Discussion guide

A 45-minute session, built to be run by a teacher who has not read the book.

Two archives, and the difference matters

Your download holds two zip files. The complete archive has all eight documents. The student archive has only the four a student should see: the specimen record, the reader introduction, the sources and the reading companion.

That means you can hand out the student archive whole, without also handing out the three quiz variants and the answer guide. Send students that one.

What you can do with it

One teacher

It is yours. Print it, copy it, put it on your own class page, and use it with every class you teach, this year and every year after. Nothing expires, there is no seat to renew, and the packet keeps working if this site does not.

What it is not is a site licence. If another teacher in your building wants it, they need their own copy. School and district pricing is being worked out for exactly that case, and the numbers will go on the pricing page when they exist.

The book itself

Borrow it free

The packet is the part that costs money. The book does not have to. Every title on this site leads with borrowing: your public library first, then Libby, which puts library ebooks and audiobooks on a phone with a card you already have. Public domain titles are free to anyone, forever, at Project Gutenberg or Open Library. Anything you would rather own links to Bookshop.org, which sends the money to independent bookstores. Your title's page in the catalog carries all of those links, along with the full source list behind the packet and any correction posted since it was printed.
