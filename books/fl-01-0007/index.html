Jurassic Park

Michael Crichton, 1990

Primary
100A Biological Sciences · 200A Physical Sciences
Focused
101A Genetics · 202A Engineering

A lawyer named Gennaro and a paleontologist named Alan Grant are flown out to Isla Nublar, an island off the Pacific coast of Costa Rica that John Hammond's company, InGen, has quietly bought and stocked. Hammond's geneticists, led by Henry Wu, have recovered dinosaur DNA from biting insects preserved in amber, patched the gaps in the broken sequences with frog DNA, and grown the animals on a resort island that has not opened yet. Grant brings the paleobotanist Ellie Sattler, Hammond brings his grandchildren Tim and Lex, and the mathematician Ian Malcolm comes along to argue, at length and in advance, that the park is a nonlinear system and is already failing. Every safeguard is a number in a computer: the electrified fences, the animal counts that only ever look for the expected total, and a lysine deficiency engineered into the animals so they cannot live off the island's food. Dennis Nedry, the contractor who wrote that computer system, shuts it down to steal frozen embryos, and what follows is not one accident but a cascade. The velociraptors are the ones that hunt. The novel ends with Grant held in Costa Rica by a government that has noticed unknown animals moving inland from the coast in straight lines, eating lysine-rich crops.

Where to find the book

Borrow it free

  • Your public library. Almost every branch in the English-speaking world holds this one, usually in several copies and several formats. If yours is out, the hold will be short. Interlibrary loan is free if your branch does not carry it.

  • Libby. libbyapp.com. Library ebooks and audiobooks on a phone or tablet, free with a library card. Both formats are widely licensed for this title, so a wait is unlikely.

  • Open Library. openlibrary.org. Free borrowing accounts, one copy out at a time, no library card needed.

  • Your school library. More likely to hold this than anything else in the Core Library. Ask first; it is often on a shelf nobody has checked.

Read free, legally

  • Not available. The novel was published in 1990 and is in copyright. Anything offering a free download is pirating it. The library is the free route, and for this title it is an easy one.

Buy it

  • Bookshop.org. bookshop.org. Orders support independent bookshops rather than a warehouse. Widely in print in paperback.

  • Your local bookshop. They can order it in a day or two if it is not on the shelf, and the money stays in your town.

  • Secondhand. This book sold in enormous numbers, which means used copies are everywhere and cheap. Charity shops, library sales and secondhand dealers almost always have one.

Sources

17 in total

Everything in the packet is built on real research, and most of it is free. If a claim sparks a question, go to the source and check it yourself. That is the whole idea.

Start here

  • Cano, Raúl J., et al. "Amplification and Sequencing of DNA from a 120-135-Million-Year-Old Weevil." Nature, vol. 363, 1993, pp. 536-538.

    The paper that appeared to prove the novel right, published three years after it and the same month the film opened.

  • Austin, Jeremy J., et al. "Problems of Reproducibility: Does Geologically Ancient DNA Survive in Amber-Preserved Insects?" Proceedings of the Royal Society B, vol. 264, 1997, pp. 467-474.

    What happened next. Careful, unexcited and devastating: they tried to repeat the amber result and could not.

  • Allentoft, Morten E., et al. "The Half-Life of DNA in Bone: Measuring Decay Kinetics in 158 Dated Fossils." Proceedings of the Royal Society B, vol. 279, no. 1748, 2012, pp. 4724-4733.

    The paper that ends the argument. Moa bones give the first real measurement of how fast DNA falls apart: a half-life of about 521 years, and nothing readable past roughly 1.5 million even in ideal cold.

The world the novel came out of

  • Diamond v. Chakrabarty, 447 U.S. 303 (1980).

    The Supreme Court decides, five to four, that a living organism modified by human beings can be patented. Short, readable, and the legal floor under InGen.

  • Gleick, James. Chaos: Making a New Science. Viking, 1987.

    The book that put chaos theory in front of a general readership three years before this novel, and which Crichton credits in his acknowledgments. Go to this before any textbook.

  • Poinar, George, and Roberta Poinar. The Quest for Life in Amber. Addison-Wesley, 1994.

    By one of the authors of the 1993 paper, written at the height of the excitement. A period document now, and a lesson in how confident a field can sound before anyone has replicated it.

The author

  • Crichton, Michael. "Aliens Cause Global Warming." Michelin Lecture, California Institute of Technology, 17 Jan. 2003.

    The lecture attacking the idea of scientific consensus. Read it directly rather than reading about it, then read the next entry.

  • Brigham-Grette, Julie, et al. "Petroleum Geologists' Award to Novelist Crichton Is Inappropriate." Eos, vol. 87, no. 36, 2006, p. 364.

    The scientific community answering in its own journal. Four hundred words, and a model of how a discipline states an objection.
